Weight and balance

Every flight must stay under the airplane's maximum weight with its center of gravity (CG) inside the approved envelope. The math is only multiplication and division, and the written test always includes a few problems.

Zarhart study notes · Sources listed below · Updated October 2026

The vocabulary

  • Datum: an imaginary vertical plane chosen by the manufacturer. Every arm is measured from it, positive aft.
  • Arm: the horizontal distance from the datum to an item, in inches.
  • Moment: weight × arm, in pound-inches.
  • Center of gravity: total moment ÷ total weight. The point where the airplane would balance.
  • Basic empty weight: the airplane with its fixed equipment, unusable fuel and full engine oil.
  • Useful load: maximum weight minus basic empty weight: the people, baggage and usable fuel you can add.

The two formulasMoment = weight × arm  ·  CG = total moment ÷ total weight

Worked example: find the CG

ItemWeight (lb)Arm (in)Moment (lb-in)
Basic empty weight1,50038.057,000
Pilot and front passenger34037.012,580
Rear passenger18073.013,140
Fuel, 40 gal × 6 lb24048.011,520
Baggage5095.04,750
Total2,31042.998,990

CG = 98,990 ÷ 2,310 ≈ 42.9 inches aft of the datum. Now check it against the POH's CG envelope for 2,310 lb. If the maximum weight is 2,300 lb, this airplane is 10 lb too heavy: moving things around changes the CG but not the total weight, so something has to come out.

Moving weight to fix the CG

Weight shiftweight moved ÷ total weight = CG change ÷ distance moved

Example: the airplane weighs 2,400 lb with the CG at 45.0 in, and the aft limit is 44.0 in. How much baggage must move from the 120-in station to the 40-in station? The CG must move 1.0 in and the baggage moves 80 in, so weight = 2,400 × 1.0 ÷ 80 = 30 lb.

Why it matters in the air

ConditionEffect
CG forwardMore stable, but the tail must push down harder: higher stall speed, slower cruise, and you may run out of elevator in the flare.
CG aftLess stable, lighter controls and a lower stall speed, but stall and spin recovery can become difficult or impossible.
OverweightLonger takeoff, weaker climb, higher stall and landing speeds, and extra stress on the structure.

Fuel burn moves the CG too

As fuel burns, its weight leaves from the tanks' arm. If the tanks are aft of the CG, the CG moves forward during the flight; if they're forward, it moves aft. Check both the takeoff and the landing CG. Standard weights: avgas 6 lb/gal, oil 7.5 lb/gal, water 8.35 lb/gal.

  1. Given: empty weight 1,500 lb at arm 38 in; front seats 340 lb at 37 in; rear seat 180 lb at 73 in; fuel 240 lb at 48 in; baggage 50 lb at 95 in. What is the CG?

    1. AAbout 40.2 in aft of datum
    2. BAbout 45.6 in aft of datum
    3. CAbout 42.9 in aft of datum
    Show answer

    C. About 42.9 in aft of datum

    Moments: 57,000 + 12,580 + 13,140 + 11,520 + 4,750 = 98,990 lb-in. Weight: 2,310 lb. CG = 98,990 ÷ 2,310 ≈ 42.9 in.

    1. A24 lb
    2. B30 lb
    3. C60 lb
    Show answer

    B. 30 lb

    Weight shifted ÷ total weight = CG change ÷ distance moved. W = 2,400 × 1.0 ÷ 80 = 30 lb.

  3. Fuel is stored at an arm aft of the airplane's loaded CG. As fuel burns, the CG

    1. Amoves aft
    2. Bdoesn't move
    3. Cmoves forward
    Show answer

    C. moves forward

    Removing weight that is aft of the CG moves the CG forward. Check that the CG stays within limits for the whole flight.

  4. An airplane loaded with the CG forward of the forward limit will

    1. Ahave a higher stall speed and be harder to flare
    2. Bstall at a lower speed
    3. Cbecome less stable
    Show answer

    A. have a higher stall speed and be harder to flare

    A forward CG needs more tail-down force: more effective weight, higher stall speed, and elevator authority may run out in the flare.
