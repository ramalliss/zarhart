Airport signs and markings

Every sign and line on an airport tells you where you are, where you're going, or where you must stop. Learn the color code and a handful of markings and you can taxi anywhere, and answer the written test's airport-diagram questions.

Zarhart study notes · Sources listed below · Updated October 2026

Sign colors

SignLooks likeTells you
Mandatory instruction15-33Red with white text: a runway entrance, a critical area, or an area you may not enter. At a towered airport you need a clearance to go past it.
LocationBBlack with yellow text and a yellow border: the taxiway or runway you're on.
Direction← AYellow with black text and arrows: the taxiways leading out of the next intersection.
Destination↑ 22Yellow with black text and an arrow: the route to a destination. Outbound destination signs point to departure runways; inbound ones point to places like the apron or terminal.

Runway distance remaining3Black with a white number: thousands of feet of runway left.

Runway holding position markings

Four yellow lines across the taxiway: two solid and two dashed. The solid lines are always on the side where you hold. Approaching a runway, you meet the solid lines first: stop there until you're cleared (at a towered airport) or until it's safe (at a nontowered one). Leaving a runway, you meet the dashed lines first, and you're not clear of the runway until every part of the airplane has crossed the marking.

Runway markings

  • Colors. Runway markings are white. Taxiway markings, holding positions and closed or hazardous areas are yellow.
  • Runway numbers. The number is the runway's magnetic direction divided by 10 and rounded: runway 27 points about 270° magnetic, and its other end is runway 9.
  • Displaced threshold. White arrows lead up to a white threshold bar. The pavement behind it can be used to taxi, to take off in either direction, and for the landing rollout from the opposite direction, but you can't touch down there when landing toward the threshold.
  • Chevrons. Yellow chevrons mark pavement in line with the runway, like a blast pad, that can't be used for landing, takeoff or taxiing.
  • Closed runway or taxiway. Yellow crosses (X) at each end.

  2. At runway hold-short markings, which side do you stop on?

    1. AThe side with the two SOLID yellow lines
    2. BThe side with the two dashed lines
    3. CEither side
    Show answer

    A. The side with the two SOLID yellow lines

    Solid lines facing you mean stop. Dashed lines facing you mean you can cross them when exiting the runway.

  3. What does the outbound destination sign identify?

    1. ADirections to takeoff runways
    2. BDirections to the ramp, terminal, or FBO
    3. CThe taxiway you are currently on
    Show answer

    A. Directions to takeoff runways

    Destination signs are yellow with black text and an arrow. Outbound signs point to takeoff runways; inbound signs point to the ramp or FBO. A black sign with yellow text is a location sign: where you ARE.

    AIM 2-3-11 · Open in Zarhart Prep

  4. What is the purpose of the runway/runway hold position sign?

    1. ADenotes intersecting runways
    2. BDenotes the entrance to a runway from a taxiway
    3. CDenotes the area protected for an aircraft approaching or departing a runway
    Show answer

    A. Denotes intersecting runways

    It's a red sign with white text like '15-33', placed on a runway where another runway crosses it. You stop there only when ATC says so (for example, LAHSO).
