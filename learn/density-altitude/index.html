Density altitude

Density altitude is the altitude your airplane performs as if it were at. On a hot day at a high airport the air is thin, so the wing, the propeller and the engine all act as though you were thousands of feet higher.

Zarhart study notes · Sources listed below · Updated October 2026

Start with pressure altitude

Pressure altitude is what the altimeter reads with 29.92 inHg set in the Kollsman window. From a field elevation and an altimeter setting:

Pressure altitudefield elevation + (29.92 − altimeter setting) × 1,000

A higher altimeter setting gives a lower pressure altitude. Example: field elevation 1,000 ft and altimeter setting 30.42 gives 1,000 + (29.92 − 30.42) × 1,000 = 500 ft.

Then correct for temperature

Density altitude is pressure altitude corrected for nonstandard temperature. Standard temperature is 15 °C at sea level and drops about 2 °C per 1,000 ft. A common rule of thumb:

Density altitude, roughlypressure altitude + 120 × (outside air temperature − standard temperature)

Example: an airport at 5,000 ft pressure altitude on a 30 °C day. Standard temperature at 5,000 ft is about 15 − 10 = 5 °C, so the air is 25 °C warmer than standard. Density altitude ≈ 5,000 + 120 × 25 = 8,000 ft. For planning, use a density altitude chart or your airplane's performance charts; the rule of thumb is a quick check.

High, hot and humid

  • What raises it: higher elevation, higher temperature and higher humidity. Humid air is less dense than dry air because water vapor is lighter than the nitrogen and oxygen it replaces.
  • What it costs you: a longer takeoff roll, a lower rate of climb and less engine power and propeller efficiency.
  • What stays the same: the indicated airspeeds you fly. You still rotate and approach at the same indicated speeds, but your true airspeed and groundspeed are higher, so you use more runway.
  • What to do: check the takeoff and climb charts in your POH, lean the mixture as it directs at high-elevation airports, and consider waiting for the cooler morning.

  1. What effect does high density altitude have on aircraft performance?

    1. AIt improves engine performance
    2. BIt reduces performance: longer takeoff roll, lower climb rate, less engine power
    3. CIt has no effect on takeoff distance
    Show answer

    B. It reduces performance: longer takeoff roll, lower climb rate, less engine power

    Thin air hurts the wing, the propeller, and the engine at the same time. High, hot, and humid all raise density altitude.

    High density altitude means poor performance (Q2).

  3. What is the pressure altitude at an airport with 1,000 ft elevation and an altimeter setting of 30.42?

    3. C1,000 ft

    B. 500 ft

    (29.92 − 30.42) × 1,000 = −500. Then 1,000 − 500 = 500 ft. A higher setting gives a lower pressure altitude.
