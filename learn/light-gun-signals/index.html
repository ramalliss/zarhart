Light gun signals

If your radio fails near a towered airport, the tower can still talk to you with a light gun. There are six signals, each with one meaning on the ground and another in the air. They're a written-test favorite because two of them are easy to swap.

Zarhart study notes · Sources listed below · Updated October 2026

The six signals

SignalOn the groundIn flight
Steady greenCleared for takeoffCleared to land
Flashing greenCleared to taxiReturn for landing (a steady green follows when it's time to land)
Steady redStopGive way to other aircraft and continue circling
Flashing redTaxi clear of the runway in useAirport unsafe: do not land
Flashing whiteReturn to starting point on the airportNot applicable
Alternating red and greenExercise extreme cautionExercise extreme caution
The one most people missSteady red in the air is about traffic: give way and keep circling. Flashing red in the air is about the airport: it's unsafe, so don't land there at all. On the ground, steady red means stop and flashing red means get off the runway.

Acknowledging the tower

  • In flight, by day: rock your wings.
  • On the ground, by day: move your ailerons or rudder.
  • At night: blink your landing or navigation lights.

If your radio fails

  1. Set your transponder to 7600.
  2. Stay outside or above the Class D surface area until you've worked out the direction and flow of traffic.
  3. Join the traffic pattern and watch the tower for light signals. If only your receiver has failed, you can still tell the tower your position and that you'd like to be controlled with light signals.
  4. Acknowledge each signal, and land only on a steady green.

If your radio fails before you leave the parking area at a towered airport, try to get it fixed. If you can't, call the tower by phone and ask for permission to depart without two-way radio.

Practice questions

From the Zarhart question bank, written from FAA references in the style of the knowledge test. Pick an answer, then open it to check.

  1. Light gun signals to an aircraft ON THE GROUND

    1. ASteady green: taxi. Flashing green: takeoff. Steady red: taxi clear
    2. BSteady green: cleared for takeoff. Flashing green: cleared to taxi. Steady red: stop. Flashing red: taxi clear of the runway in use. Flashing white: return to starting point
    3. CFlashing red: stop. Steady red: return to starting point
    Show answer

    B. Steady green: cleared for takeoff. Flashing green: cleared to taxi. Steady red: stop. Flashing red: taxi clear of the runway in use. Flashing white: return to starting point

    Alternating red and green means exercise extreme caution, both on the ground and in flight.

    14 CFR 91.125 · Open in Zarhart Prep

  2. Light gun signals to an aircraft IN FLIGHT

    1. ASteady red: airport unsafe. Flashing red: give way and circle
    2. BFlashing green: cleared to land
    3. CSteady green: cleared to land. Flashing green: return for landing. Steady red: give way and keep circling. Flashing red: airport unsafe, do not land
    Show answer

    C. Steady green: cleared to land. Flashing green: return for landing. Steady red: give way and keep circling. Flashing red: airport unsafe, do not land

    The steady red vs. flashing red difference is one of the most missed items.

    14 CFR 91.125 · Open in Zarhart Prep

  3. Transponder codes to know

    1. A1200 = emergency, 7700 = VFR
    2. B7600 = hijack, 7500 = lost communications
    3. C1200 = VFR, 7500 = hijack, 7600 = lost communications, 7700 = emergency
    Show answer

    C. 1200 = VFR, 7500 = hijack, 7600 = lost communications, 7700 = emergency

    Memory aid: 75 taken alive, 76 radio fix, 77 going to heaven.
