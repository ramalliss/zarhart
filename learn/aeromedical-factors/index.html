Aeromedical factors

Your body is part of the system. The written test checks that you can recognize hypoxia, disorientation and the illusions that put airplanes into the ground short of the runway, and that you know the rules on alcohol, drugs and diving.

Zarhart study notes · Sources listed below · Updated October 2026

Hypoxia: not enough oxygen

TypeCauseExample
HypoxicNot enough oxygen in the air you breatheFlying at altitude without supplemental oxygen
HypemicThe blood can't carry enough oxygenCarbon monoxide poisoning, anemia, recent blood donation
StagnantThe blood isn't circulating wellHigh G-loads, cold extremities, heart problems
HistotoxicThe cells can't use the oxygenAlcohol and some drugs

Symptoms creep in unnoticed: euphoria, headache, slower reactions, poor judgment, tunnel vision and blue lips or fingernails. The fix is oxygen and a descent. 14 CFR 91.211 requires the crew to use oxygen above 12,500 ft after 30 minutes and at all times above 14,000 ft, and every occupant must be offered it above 15,000 ft.

Hyperventilation and carbon monoxide

  • Hyperventilation is breathing too fast, often from stress or anxiety, which flushes out too much carbon dioxide. Lightheadedness, tingling and muscle spasms follow. Slow your breathing, talk out loud or breathe into a bag.
  • Carbon monoxide is colorless and odorless and often leaks from a cracked exhaust into the cabin heat. Headache, drowsiness or dizziness? Turn the heater off, open the fresh-air vents, use oxygen if you have it and land.

Spatial disorientation

Without a visible horizon the inner ear is easily fooled. Trust the instruments.

IllusionWhat happens
The leansAfter a long, gentle turn, rolling level feels like banking the other way, so you lean or re-enter the turn.
Graveyard spiralIn a prolonged descending turn you feel level; pulling back without leveling the wings tightens the spiral.
Coriolis illusionMoving your head abruptly during a turn gives an overwhelming sense of tumbling.
SomatogravicA rapid acceleration on takeoff feels like a nose-up pitch, tempting you to push the nose down.
False horizonSloping cloud tops or ground lights are mistaken for the horizon.
AutokinesisA single point of light stared at in the dark seems to move.

Landing illusions

ConditionIllusionTypical error
Narrower runway than usualYou seem higherA low approach
Wider runway than usualYou seem lowerA high approach
Upsloping runwayYou seem higherA low approach
Downsloping runwayYou seem lowerA high approach
Featureless terrain, water or a dark night (black hole)You seem higherA low approach
Rain on the windscreenYou seem higherA low approach
HazeThe runway seems farther awayA low approach

Most of these push you low, the most dangerous direction. A VASI or PAPI and a stabilized approach are the antidote.

Night vision

  • The cones in the center of the retina handle color and detail but work poorly in dim light. The rods around them see in the dark, so look slightly off-center at a dim object.
  • Full dark adaptation takes about 30 minutes, and one bright light ruins it. Use dim cockpit lighting.
  • Night vision suffers early from a lack of oxygen, even at moderate altitudes.

Alcohol, drugs and diving

  • Alcohol: no flying within 8 hours of a drink, while under the influence, or with a blood alcohol concentration of 0.04 or more (14 CFR 91.17). Many pilots use 12 to 24 hours.
  • Medication: no flying under the influence of any drug that affects your faculties. Antihistamines and sleep aids are common culprits; ask an aviation medical examiner.
  • Scuba diving: wait 12 hours before flying up to 8,000 ft after a dive that didn't need a controlled ascent, and 24 hours before flying higher or after a dive that did (AIM 8-1-2).
  • IMSAFE: before each flight check Illness, Medication, Stress, Alcohol, Fatigue and Emotion.

Practice questions

From the Zarhart question bank, written from FAA references in the style of the knowledge test. Pick an answer, then open it to check.

  1. Hypoxic hypoxia is caused by

    1. Acarbon monoxide poisoning
    2. Bthe low partial pressure of oxygen at altitude
    3. Cthe effects of alcohol
    Show answer

    B. the low partial pressure of oxygen at altitude

    Hypoxic: not enough oxygen available (altitude). Hypemic: blood can't carry it (CO, anemia). Stagnant: poor circulation (G-forces, cold). Histotoxic: cells can't use it (alcohol, drugs).

  2. Carbon monoxide poisoning causes which type of hypoxia?

    1. AHypemic
    2. BHypoxic
    3. CHistotoxic
    Show answer

    A. Hypemic

    CO binds to hemoglobin far more readily than oxygen, so the blood can't carry enough oxygen.

    PHAK ch. 17 · Open in Zarhart Prep

  3. A runway that is narrower than usual can make you fly a final approach that is

    1. Alower than normal, because you think you're too high
    2. Bhigher than normal
    3. Cfaster than normal
    Show answer

    A. lower than normal, because you think you're too high

    A narrow runway looks like a higher approach; you may descend too low. A wide runway has the opposite effect.

    PHAK ch. 17 · Open in Zarhart Prep

  4. At night, the most effective way to see a dim object is to look

    1. Adirectly at the object
    2. Bslightly off-center from the object
    3. Cat the object through a scanning motion only
    Show answer

    B. slightly off-center from the object

    The rods, which work best in dim light, are around the edge of the retina, not in the center.
