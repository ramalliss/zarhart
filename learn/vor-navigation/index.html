VOR navigation

A VOR sends out 360 radials like the spokes of a wheel, each one a magnetic bearing from the station. With the OBS knob, the course deviation needle and the TO/FROM flag you can find which radial you're on and fly to or from the station along any of them.

Zarhart study notes · Sources listed below · Updated October 2026

Radials and courses

A radial is a magnetic bearing from the station. If you're on the 090 radial you are east of the station. To fly inbound on the 090 radial you steer a course of 270, toward the station; outbound on the 090 radial you steer 090.

Inbound vs. outboundInbound on a radial: fly its reciprocal with a TO flag. Outbound on a radial: fly the radial itself with a FROM flag.

Reading the instrument

  • OBS (omni bearing selector): selects the course you want to fly.
  • CDI (course deviation indicator): shows where that course is. When your heading roughly agrees with the selected course, fly toward the needle. Full-scale deflection is 10° or more; on a five-dot display each dot is 2°.
  • TO/FROM flag: whether the selected course would take you toward or away from the station. It doesn't show which way you're heading.
  • OFF (or red) flag: the signal is unusable: out of range, too close to the station, or the station is down.
  • Reverse sensing: fly a heading roughly opposite to the selected course and the needle points the wrong way. Set the OBS to the course you're actually flying.

Where am I?

Turn the OBS until the needle centers with a FROM flag: the number under the index is the radial you're on. Do the same with a second VOR and draw both radials on the chart. Where they cross is your position.

Intercepting and tracking

  • Set the OBS to the course, turn to intercept it at 30° to 45°, and start the turn onto course as the needle moves toward center.
  • In a crosswind, bracket: hold a heading with a wind correction, see which way the needle drifts, and halve the correction until it stays put.

Identify it, and know its limits

  • Always identify the station by its three-letter Morse code before using it. No identifier can mean it's in maintenance and unreliable.
  • VOR is line-of-sight, so range shrinks at low altitude and behind terrain.
  • DME shows slant range in nautical miles, so it overstates the horizontal distance when you're high and close to the station.

VOR receiver checks

CheckMaximum errorHow
VOT (VOR test signal)±4°Center the needle: 360 with a FROM flag, or 180 with a TO flag
VOR ground checkpoint±4°At the marked spot on the airport, against the published radial
Airborne checkpoint±6°Over a published landmark, against the published radial
Dual VOR check4° between the twoTune both to the same station and compare

14 CFR 91.171 requires a check within the preceding 30 days for IFR flight, logged with the date, place, bearing error and a signature. It's good practice for VFR too.

  1. A VOR radial is

    1. Aa magnetic bearing extending outward from the VOR station
    2. Ba true bearing toward the station
    3. Ca course defined by two VORs
    Show answer

    A. a magnetic bearing extending outward from the VOR station

    Radials are FROM the station, referenced to magnetic north. Flying inbound on the 090 radial means a course of 270.

    2. B150°
    3. C060°

    C. 060°

    Inbound on the 240 radial, you fly the reciprocal: 060°, with a TO flag.
