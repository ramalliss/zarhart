Pilot-controlled lighting

At many airports without an operating tower, the runway lights stay off until a pilot turns them on from the cockpit by keying the mic on the right frequency. Here's how it works, and the other airport lights the written test asks about.

Zarhart study notes · Sources listed below · Updated October 2026

The clicks

Key the micYou get
7 times within 5 secondsHighest intensity available
5 times within 5 secondsMedium or lower intensity (lower REIL or REIL off)
3 times within 5 secondsLowest intensity available (lower REIL or REIL off)
  • Always start with 7. That turns every controlled light on at full intensity. Then step down with 5 or 3 clicks if the system allows it.
  • 15 minutes. The lights stay on for 15 minutes from the most recent activation. Key the mic again when you overfly the airport or just before final, so you're close enough to trigger it and get a full 15 minutes for the landing.
  • One frequency. All the radio-controlled lighting at an airport works on the same frequency, often the CTAF. The FAA Chart Supplement lists the lighting and the frequency, and approach charts show it too.
  • Get close. The receivers may be set to low sensitivity, so you might have to be fairly near the airport.

Other airport lights on the test

LightColor
Rotating beacon, lighted land airportWhite and green
Rotating beacon, lighted water airportWhite and yellow
Rotating beacon, military airportTwo quick white flashes between the green flashes
Rotating beacon, lighted heliportGreen, yellow and white
Taxiway edge lightsBlue
Taxiway centerline lightsGreen
Taxiway centerline lead-off lightsAlternating green and yellow while you're still in the runway environment
Runway guard lightsYellow: a flashing pair beside the taxiway, or a row across it, at the runway holding position

In Class B, C, D and E surface areas, a beacon that's on in daylight often means the ground visibility is under 3 miles or the ceiling is under 1,000 ft. Don't rely on the beacon alone to tell you whether it's VFR.

Practice questions

From the Zarhart question bank, written from FAA references in the style of the knowledge test. Pick an answer, then open it to check.

  1. How many times should you key the mic on the specified frequency at a nontowered airport to turn on pilot-controlled lighting at highest intensity?

    1. A5 times within 5 seconds
    2. B3 times within 3 seconds
    3. C7 times within 5 seconds
    Show answer

    C. 7 times within 5 seconds

    7 clicks = high, 5 = medium, 3 = low, all within 5 seconds. The AIM says to key 7 times first, then step down. The lights stay on about 15 minutes.

    AIM 2-1-8 · Open in Zarhart Prep

  2. What does a civilian land airport's rotating beacon flash?

    1. AWhite and green
    2. BWhite, white, and green
    3. CWhite and yellow
    Show answer

    A. White and green

    Two whites then green = military field. Yellow = water airport. A beacon on in daylight in Class B, C, D or E surface areas can mean below-VFR weather (ceiling under 1,000 ft or visibility under 3 SM).

    AIM 2-1-9 · Open in Zarhart Prep

  3. What color are taxiway lead-off lights leaving a runway?

    1. ABlue
    2. BAlternating green and yellow
    3. CAlternating red and white
    Show answer

    B. Alternating green and yellow

    Taxiway edge lights are blue, taxiway centerline lights are green, and lead-off lights alternate green and yellow.

    AIM 2-1-5 · Open in Zarhart Prep
