VFR weather minimums

How far you must be able to see and how far you must stay from clouds depends on the airspace class, your altitude and whether it's day or night. Here's the whole table from 14 CFR 91.155 and how the written test asks about it.

The Class G values are the ones for airplanes. Helicopters have their own, lower minimums.

Memory aid“3-152”: 3 SM visibility, 1,000 ft above, 500 ft below and 2,000 ft horizontal from clouds. That covers Class C, Class D, Class E below 10,000 ft MSL and Class G at night. Then learn the exceptions: Class B is 3 SM and clear of clouds, Class G near the surface by day is 1 SM and clear of clouds, and at or above 10,000 ft MSL it's 5 SM with 1,000 ft above and below and 1 SM horizontal.

The rules around the table

  • The 1,000 ft ceiling rule. Inside the surface area of controlled airspace for an airport, you may not fly VFR beneath the ceiling when the ceiling is less than 1,000 ft (91.155(c)).
  • 3 SM to take off, land or enter the pattern. In a Class B, C, D or E surface area you need ground visibility of at least 3 SM. If ground visibility isn't reported, you need flight visibility of at least 3 SM (91.155(d)).
  • The night pattern exception. In Class G below 1,200 ft AGL at night, an airplane in the traffic pattern within ½ mile of the runway may fly clear of clouds with at least 1 SM visibility (91.155(b)(2)).
  • The base of Class E counts as below it. Flying exactly at the floor of a Class E area, you're treated as being in the airspace underneath (91.155(e)).

Special VFR

When the weather is below basic VFR minimums in the surface area of controlled airspace, ATC can issue a special VFR clearance (14 CFR 91.157):

  • Only below 10,000 ft MSL, inside the airspace above the surface area designated for an airport.
  • You need an ATC clearance. You have to ask for it.
  • Stay clear of clouds with at least 1 SM flight visibility. To take off or land you need 1 SM ground visibility, or 1 SM flight visibility if ground visibility isn't reported.
  • Between sunset and sunrise, only if you meet the requirements for instrument flight and the airplane is equipped for IFR (Alaska has its own rule).
  • Not available at some of the busiest Class B airports, listed in Part 91, appendix D, section 3.

How the test asks about it

Expect a question that gives you an altitude and an airspace and asks for the visibility or cloud clearance, often with a sectional chart figure where you first have to work out which airspace you're in. Read for the details that change the answer: day or night, above or below 1,200 ft AGL, and above or below 10,000 ft MSL.
